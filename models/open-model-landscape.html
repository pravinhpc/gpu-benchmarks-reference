Open Model Landscape

Cross-vendor comparison of major open-weight LLM families — licenses, architectures, benchmarks, and what actually matters when picking one — updated 2026-09-09. Public web research only — no NVIDIA-internal content.

1. What is this, and why does it matter?

"Open-weight" doesn't mean one thing — licenses range from truly permissive (Apache 2.0, MIT) to gated/custom terms with usage caps. This page tracks the major families people actually deploy against each other: what they cost to run, what license risk they carry, and how they're architected (dense vs. Mixture-of-Experts).

Benchmark numbers in the open-model space move fast and are frequently gamed (see the Llama 4 Maverick LMArena controversy in §5). Treat any single leaderboard number as a snapshot, not gospel — cross-check against the primary source (official model card / official leaderboard site) before using a number to make a real decision.

2. Model families

Meta Llama (Llama 4)

FlagshipLlama 4 Maverick — 400B total / 17B active (128 experts), MoE. Llama 4 Scout — 109B total / 17B active (16 experts).
ContextMaverick 1M tokens; Scout 10M tokens.
Licensecustom Llama 4 Community License — free commercial use except companies >700M MAU (need separate Meta license); EU restriction on multimodal features. Not OSI-approved open source despite "open" branding.
BenchmarksMeta's style-tuned "03-26-Experimental" variant scored 1417 Elo on LMArena; the actual public release ranks far lower (~32nd) once style-control is applied — a widely-cited benchmark-gaming controversy.
Why it mattersLargest tooling/inference-stack ecosystem of any open-weight family, despite the non-open license.
Linksgithub.com/meta-llama/llama-models

Alibaba Qwen (Qwen3 / Qwen3.6)

FlagshipQwen3-235B-A22B (235B total / 22B active, MoE); dense variants also offered (32B/8B/4B/0.6B). Newer Qwen3.6-35B-A3B.
Context32,768 native, extendable to 131,072 via YaRN (Qwen3); Qwen3.6-35B-A3B up to ~1M via extension.
LicenseApache 2.0 across the family — one of the most permissive of the major players.
Why it mattersStrong multilingual performance + fully permissive license drives heavy enterprise/community adoption. Both dense and MoE variants let you pick the right shape for your deployment.
Linkshuggingface.co/Qwen, github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3

DeepSeek (V3 / R1)

FlagshipDeepSeek-V3 — 671B total / 37B active, MoE with Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) + auxiliary-loss-free load balancing. R1 = V3-Base + RL for reasoning.
Context128K tokens.
LicenseMIT (code); model weights under a separate DeepSeek license permitting commercial use.
Training cost claim~2.788M H800 GPU-hours, reported at roughly $5.6M — the claim that triggered the "DeepSeek moment" in the industry (proof frontier-class reasoning could be trained far cheaper than assumed).
BenchmarksR1 vs OpenAI o1: AIME 2024 79.8% vs 79.2% (R1 ahead); MATH-500 97.3% (R1 leads); MMLU 90.8% vs 91.8% (o1 slightly ahead). Source figures aggregated from secondary comparisons — verify against the original arXiv paper before quoting.
Linksgithub.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3, huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1

Google Gemma (Gemma 3)

Sizes270M, 1B, 4B, 12B, 27B — dense transformer, multimodal (text+image in, text out) at larger sizes.
Context128K tokens. 140+ languages supported.
Licensecustom Gemma Terms of Use — gated on Hugging Face, requires accepting Google's usage terms; not a standard OSI license.
Why it mattersStrong small-model efficiency line (270M-27B) aimed squarely at on-device/edge deployment.
Linkshuggingface.co/google/gemma-3-27b-it

Mistral (Large 3 / historical Mixtral)

FlagshipMistral Large 3 (Dec 2025) — 675B total, ~39-41B active (sources vary — confirm on HF card), MoE.
Context256K tokens.
LicenseApache 2.0 — a shift to full permissiveness for the flagship release (verify on HF card before relying on this).
HistoricalMixtral 8x7B (45B total/13B active) and 8x22B (176B total/44B active) — both Apache 2.0, early proof points for open MoE.
Why it mattersEuropean lab; first to popularize open MoE via Mixtral; Large 3 returns to fully permissive licensing at frontier scale.
Linkshuggingface.co/mistralai

Zhipu GLM (GLM-4.6 / GLM-5.x)

FlagshipGLM-5 — 744B total / 40B active, MoE, 200K context. GLM-5.2 extends to ~1M context for agentic/long-horizon coding. GLM-4.6 — 355B total MoE, 200K context.
LicenseMIT across the family — notably permissive for frontier-scale (700B+) models.
BenchmarksGLM-5.3-Flash reported top-ranked open-source model on Artificial Analysis (score 0.570) — single third-party snapshot, not independently cross-verified here; pull live from artificialanalysis.ai before quoting.
Why it mattersChinese lab producing some of the largest fully MIT-licensed open-weight models; strong coding/agentic positioning.
Linkshuggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-4.6

xAI Grok (open-weight releases only)

Grok-1314B MoE, released under Apache 2.0 — genuinely open source.
Grok-2Released under the more restrictive "Grok 2 Community License" — custom, research-first, commercial use gated behind xAI guardrails.
FlagGrok is the one family here that moved from fully permissive to more restrictive licensing between releases — a license-trend counter-example worth noting. No open-weight release of the current frontier Grok model was found; only Grok-1 and Grok-2 have public weights.
Linkshuggingface.co/xai-org/grok-1, huggingface.co/xai-org/grok-2

Other notable families

Microsoft Phi-4

Dense, MIT. Phi-4 base = 14B/16K context; Phi-4-mini = 3.8B/128K; Phi-4-Reasoning-Vision-15B (Mar 2026) = 15B/16K. "Small model punches above its weight" story.

Allen Institute OLMo

Apache 2.0, the most transparent family here — ships pretraining data + training/eval code + checkpoints + logs, not just weights. OLMo 3 (Nov 2025): 7B and 32B variants.

TII Falcon

Apache 2.0, early entrant from UAE's Technology Innovation Institute — historically important, less prominent in 2026 rankings.

01.AI Yi

Apache 2.0, 6B/9B/34B — noted for strong bilingual Chinese/English performance.

Moonshot Kimi K3

New entrant (weights released 2026-07-27). Reported #1 on LMArena's Frontend Code Arena (1,679 Elo) — first open model to top a coding leaderboard. Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index ~57.

4. Architecture: dense vs. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)

Dense

Every parameter activates on every forward pass. Simpler to serve, more predictable latency, but total params = active params = full compute cost. Examples: Gemma 3, Phi-4, OLMo 3, Qwen3 dense variants (32B/8B/4B/0.6B).

MoE

Only a subset of "expert" sub-networks activate per token — total params can be huge while active (compute) params stay small. Examples: DeepSeek-V3 (671B total/37B active), Llama 4 Maverick (400B/17B), GLM-5 (744B/40B), Mistral Large 3 (675B/~40B). This is why a 671B MoE model can be cheaper to run than its total-param count implies — but still needs enough GPU memory to hold all the experts, even though only a few fire per token.

The trend across 2025-2026: nearly every new frontier-scale open-weight release is MoE — it's become the default way to get more model capacity without proportionally more inference compute.

5. Cross-family leaderboards — and their limits

  • LMArena (LMSYS Chatbot Arena) — live Elo-based rankings from human pairwise votes. As of ~Sept 2026, proprietary models (Claude Opus, GPT-5.5 Pro, Gemini 3.1 Pro) still dominate the top overall, but open-weight Kimi K3 broke into #1 on the Frontend Code Arena sub-leaderboard specifically.
  • Artificial Analysis — Intelligence Index scoring across quality/speed/price. GLM-5.3-Flash and Kimi K3 rank near the top among open models per secondary reporting — pull live numbers directly from the site before quoting, since aggregator blogs can lag or misreport.
  • Hugging Face Open LLM Leaderboard — historically the standard reference; HF has changed/retired leaderboard formats before, so confirm it's still actively maintained before citing.
The Llama 4 Maverick episode is the cautionary tale here: Meta's submitted "03-26-Experimental" variant scored 1417 Elo (would-be top-tier), but the actual publicly released weights ranked around 32nd once LMArena applied style control — the submitted variant was tuned for benchmark performance, not representative of the shipped model. Always check whether a benchmark number was measured on the model you can actually download.

6. How to choose, practically

License-sensitive deployment

Favor Qwen3, DeepSeek, GLM-5.x, Mistral Large 3, Grok-1, Phi-4, or OLMo 3 — all Apache 2.0/MIT with no usage-count gates.

Memory-constrained serving

MoE models need enough GPU memory to hold ALL experts even if only a few fire per token — a 671B-total MoE model still needs multi-GPU memory footprint despite ~37B active compute. Dense small models (Gemma, Phi-4, Qwen3 dense) are simpler to reason about for tight memory budgets.

Full transparency / research use

OLMo 3 is the only family here shipping pretraining data + full training code + logs alongside weights — the closest thing to a fully reproducible open model.
